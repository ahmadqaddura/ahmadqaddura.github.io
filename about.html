About

Born in 1988 in a Palestinian refugee camp in Syria, Ahmad Qaddura began drawing political cartoons during the early years of the Syrian Revolution. His earliest works were inspired by Handala, the iconic character created by the late Palestinian cartoonist Naji al-Ali.

Because of one of his cartoons and his participation in the Syrian Revolution, he was pursued by the Syrian regime's security forces and forced to flee the country. He embarked on a perilous 13-day sea journey from Turkey to Europe aboard migrant boats, eventually making his way to Sweden, where he now lives.

Today, Ahmad works as a Mechanical & Piping Designer alongside his work as a freelance editorial cartoonist. He is a member of international cartoon networks and platforms including Cartooning for Peace, Cartoon Movement, and CartoonStock.

His cartoons have been published in Swedish newspapers such as Dagens Nyheter and Proletären, as well as in the Swedish anti-war satirical publication Larm.
